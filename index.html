Instagram Settings

Set Up for High Performance

These adjustments take 5 minutes and can dramatically increase your reach. Do them once.

1

Switch to a Creator Account

Settings → Account → Switch to Professional Account → Creator. Unlocks analytics, contact buttons, and category labels.

2

Turn On High-Quality Uploads

Settings → Account → Data Usage → turn on "Upload at Highest Quality." Your videos will look sharper in the feed immediately.

3

Add a Keyword to Your Name Field

Edit Profile → Name: Add your niche keyword (e.g. "Amara | Lifestyle & Wellness"). Instagram uses this field for search — very powerful.

4

Write a Bio with a Clear Call to Action

Say WHO you help, HOW you help them, and WHAT to do next (e.g. "↓ Free guide below"). Keep it under 150 characters.

5

Post at Your Peak Times

Go to Insights → Followers → scroll to "Most Active Times." Post within those hours for maximum organic reach.

6

Enable Cross-Sharing to Facebook

Settings → Account → Sharing to Other Apps → connect Facebook. Doubles your potential reach with zero extra effort.

7

Pin Your 3 Best Reels

Hold down on your top-performing Reels and tap "Pin to Profile." New visitors see your best work first — more follows guaranteed.

How to Shoot

Look Great on Camera — Every Time

Simple, practical techniques anyone can apply with just a phone. No fancy gear needed to start making great content.

📐 Camera Angles That Work ▼
  • Eye-Level: Camera at eye height — feels like a real conversation. Best for talking-head content.
  • Slightly Above Eye Level: Camera just above your face, angled slightly down. Most flattering for face and body — used by almost every creator.
  • Low Angle (Power Shot): Camera below you shooting upward. Makes you look confident and authoritative. Great for bold statements.
  • Over-the-Shoulder: Shows what you're working on or your surroundings. Adds context, makes viewers feel like they're with you.
  • Wide Shot: Shows your full body or environment. Great for outfits, room tours, cooking, or activity content.
  • Close-Up: Tight on your face or hands. Adds emotion and detail. Perfect for tutorials and reactions.
💡 Lighting Tips ▼
  • Natural light is free and the best: Face a window — don't have your back to it. Soft daylight is flattering and professional-looking.
  • Golden Hour: 1 hour after sunrise or before sunset gives warm, beautiful light outdoors. Perfect for lifestyle and fashion content.
  • Avoid harsh overhead lighting: Creates unflattering shadows under your eyes and nose.
  • Ring light tip: Position it at eye level, slightly to the side for depth. Dead centre can look flat.
  • Background lighting: Add a lamp behind you for depth — separates you from the background and looks more professional.
🎬 Framing & Composition ▼
  • Rule of Thirds: Don't always centre yourself. Position your face in the upper third of the frame — looks more dynamic.
  • Headroom: Leave a small gap between your head and the top of the frame. Too much or too little looks off.
  • Clean Background: Remove clutter. A plain wall, a shelf, or a neat space all work. Your audience should focus on YOU.
  • Create Depth: Stand a few feet in front of your background. Creates a blurred background (bokeh) that looks cinematic.
  • Always Film Vertical: Shoot in 9:16 portrait mode. Fill the whole frame — no black bars on Reels or TikTok.
🎙 Audio & Sound ▼
  • Bad audio kills good video: People will watch blurry video, but they will immediately leave if the sound is poor.
  • Quiet room first: Close windows, turn off fans, choose a room with soft furnishings — they absorb echo naturally.
  • Earphone mic trick: Plug in your basic phone earphones and speak clearly. The small inline mic is a massive free upgrade over your phone's built-in mic.
  • Music volume: Background music should sit well under your voice — around 20–30% volume max.
🎭 On-Camera Confidence ▼
  • Look at the lens, not the screen: The camera lens is the eye of your viewer. Looking there creates real connection.
  • Speak 20% more energetically than normal: Energy compresses on video. What feels big on camera often looks just right to viewers.
  • Slow down: Most new creators talk too fast. Pause between ideas — it builds tension and keeps viewers engaged.
  • Use your hands naturally: Hand gestures make you look confident and keep energy flowing in the frame.
  • Smile before you hit record: Relaxes your face and sets a warm, welcoming tone from the very first frame.
  • Do multiple takes: Your 3rd or 4th take is almost always better than your first. That's normal — not failure.
Content Planning

Post with Purpose, Not Pressure

Consistency beats frequency. Here's how many times per week actually makes sense for real growth.

1x
per week
Just Starting

Focus on quality over quantity. One great video a week builds skills and confidence without burning out.

3x
per week
Growing Stage

The sweet spot for most creators. Enough to stay visible and train the algorithm without overwhelming yourself.

5x
per week
Accelerating

Batch-record on weekends and schedule ahead. Never sacrifice quality for volume — quality still wins.

7x
per week
Full-Time Creator

Daily posting works when you have a content bank. Aim for 2–3 weeks banked before committing to daily.

Equipment

Start With What You Have

You don't need expensive gear to begin. Here's everything a beginner creator actually needs — no pressure, nothing extra.

A Good Smartphone

Your phone camera is more than enough. Any iPhone 11+ or recent Android shoots stunning video. Clean your lens before every single shoot.

✓ You likely already have this
🔭

Tripod Stand

The #1 most important purchase. Even a basic tripod from $10–$30 eliminates shaky footage and frees both your hands to create freely.

✓ From $10
💡

Ring Light

A small 6–10 inch ring light makes a massive difference to how you look on video — especially for indoor, beauty, or talking-head content.

✓ From $20
🎙

Lavalier Clip Mic

A clip-on mic that plugs into your phone. Crisp, clear audio that beats your phone's built-in mic by miles. A must for voice-heavy content.

✓ From $15
🎒

Clean Background

A plain wall, a neat shelf, or a simple $15 paper backdrop. Your background should complement you, not compete with you. Keep it consistent.

✓ Free or very cheap
📷

Selfie Stick + Tripod

A 2-in-1 selfie stick that converts to a tripod. Perfect for travel, outdoor shoots, and versatile content creation on the go.

✓ From $15
🔋

Power Bank

Nothing ruins a shoot like a dying battery. Keep a portable charger handy for long filming sessions or outdoor content days.

✓ From $15
🌬

Foam Mic Windscreen

If you shoot outdoors, wind noise destroys your audio. A simple foam cover costs $5 and eliminates wind noise completely.

✓ Under $10
☁️

Cloud Backup (Google Photos)

Auto-backup your footage so you never lose a shoot. Google Photos free tier is more than enough for most beginners starting out.

✓ Free to start
